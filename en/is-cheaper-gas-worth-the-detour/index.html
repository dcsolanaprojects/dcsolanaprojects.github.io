Is it worth driving to a cheaper gas station?

It depends on how much you save per liter and how far you have to drive. In many cases, the extra trip can erase most of the savings.

Quick rule: if the detour is long or you are only buying a small amount of fuel, it often is not worth it. If the price difference is large and the detour is short, it can make sense.

How to know if it is worth it

To know whether driving to a cheaper gas station makes sense, you need to consider four things:

  • Price difference per liter
  • How much fuel you will buy
  • Extra distance
  • Your car’s fuel consumption

The most common mistake is looking only at the fuel price without considering the cost of the extra driving.

Practical example

If you save €0.06 per liter and buy 40 liters, the gross saving is €2.40.

But if you need to drive several extra kilometers, the fuel cost of the detour — and possibly other car costs — can reduce or even eliminate that saving.

In many situations, you may end up saving less than €1 or even losing money.

When it is worth it

  • When the price difference is large
  • When you are buying a lot of fuel
  • When the detour is very small

When it is not worth it

  • When the detour is long
  • When you are only buying a small amount of fuel
  • When the price difference is minimal

Do not forget the real cost of the car

Every kilometer is not just fuel. There is also wear, maintenance, insurance and depreciation.

FAQ

How far is worth driving for cheaper fuel?

It depends on consumption, fuel amount and price difference. The calculator gives you the break-even point.

Is cheaper fuel always worth it?

No. If the detour is too long, the extra driving cost can be higher than the saving.

Is cheaper fuel worse for the car?

In most countries, fuel must meet base quality standards regardless of the station. The main difference is usually the additive package.

Some brands add proprietary detergents or additives that may help keep injectors, valves or the fuel system cleaner over time. For most everyday drivers, the short-term difference is usually small.

Myth vs reality

Myth: cheaper fuel is bad and can damage the engine.

Reality: regular fuel sold legally must meet quality standards. Differences usually come from additive packages, service model and operating costs, not from the base fuel being unsafe.

The most important thing is not only the brand, but also avoiding habits that do hurt the car, such as constantly running the tank very low or neglecting maintenance.
